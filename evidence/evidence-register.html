IPC Starter Kit v1.0.0 – Evidence & Design Register

How to Verify This Register Before Local Use:
During the offline build of this starter kit, the external source PDFs (WHO IRIS, USAID, and US HHS) could not be fetched over the network. To maintain strict citation honesty, no page numbers have been invented, expected topics/sections are listed only where confident, and every row resting on published guidance is marked "Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review". Before using the kit for real facility data, the local IPC focal person must check these claims against the facility's official copies of the source documents and sign off Part A of field-kit/ipc-lead-review-and-pilot-checklist.html.

1. Published Guidance & Kit Design Choice Claims

Note: Locally configurable Facility choice rows are separated into Table 2 below and also highlighted with a bold badge.

ID Used in Claim (our original wording) Type Source Expected section/topic Verification status Notes
HH-01 app rule / training claim Hand hygiene observation uses five moments: (1) Before touching a patient, (2) Before a clean/aseptic procedure, (3) After body fluid exposure risk, (4) After touching a patient, and (5) After touching patient surroundings. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] Five moments for hand hygiene concept & indications Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Original wording only; no WHO graphics bundled.
HH-02 app metric / app rule When one or more hand hygiene indications (moments) occur together at the same time between care tasks, they are recorded as ONE single hand hygiene opportunity and counted once. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] Definition of opportunity vs. indication during direct observation Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Prevents double-counting transition between patients.
HH-03 app metric Hand hygiene adherence (compliance) percentage is calculated as 100 multiplied by total positive actions (hand rub + handwash) divided by total opportunities. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] Compliance calculation formula Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Denominator is opportunities, not indications.
HH-04 app rule Each observed opportunity has exactly one recorded action outcome: alcohol-based hand rub, handwashing with soap and water, or missed (counted as not performed). Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] Recording hand hygiene actions (rub, wash, missed) Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review One outcome per opportunity row.
HH-05 app metric / app rule Multiple observation sessions are combined by summing action counts and opportunity counts first before calculating adherence, never by averaging session percentages. Kit design choice Follows mathematically from HH-03 (WHO TRM 2009) Aggregating observation sessions Kit design choice (mathematical rule) Tested in Scenario 6 and enforced in app & CLI tool.
HH-06 app metric / app rule Adherence is always displayed with numerator and denominator counts ('X of Y opportunities, Z%'), shows 'No observations' when Y = 0, and avoids misleading 0% or 100% rounding on non-extreme values. Kit design choice IPC Starter Kit design rule Reporting display format Kit design choice (verified in app UI) Prevents misinterpreting small denominators.
HH-08 training claim A standard direct hand hygiene observation session lasts about 20 minutes. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] Observation session duration guidance Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Verify exact session length wording during local review.
HH-09 training claim Observers should be trained and practice with sample scenarios before collecting real observation data. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] Observer training and validation Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Supported by 5-minute practice run & 6 scenarios.
HH-10 app rule / training claim Hand hygiene observation is used to improve systems and is not used to identify, blame, or judge individuals; the kit stores only grouped counts by month and area code with no names, staff categories, or timestamps. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM] + Kit design choice Observation ethics, anonymity, and non-punitive feedback Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Omitting staff category is an intentional kit privacy choice.
HH-11 training claim Wearing medical gloves does not replace hand hygiene; hands must be cleaned when indicated before putting on gloves and after removing gloves. Published guidance WHO Hand Hygiene Technical Reference Manual 2009 [TRM]; WHO Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Health Care 2009 Glove use and hand hygiene indications Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Practiced in Scenarios 2 and 3.
HH-12 training claim / checklist item Observation results are fed back regularly to health workers and facility leadership to guide practical improvements. Published guidance WHO TRM 2009; WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Performance feedback; Core Component 6 (Monitoring, audit and feedback) Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Used in 30-minute monthly review routine.
HH-13 training claim Cleaning hands with alcohol-based hand rub takes about 20–30 seconds; washing hands with soap and water takes about 40–60 seconds. Published guidance WHO Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Health Care 2009 Duration of handrubbing and handwashing procedures Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Verify exact duration range during local review.
ST-01 app rule / checklist item Each tracked IPC supply item uses one consistent counting unit (such as bottle, box of 100, or bar), which is locked after the first entry so counts never mix units. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook (2nd ed., 2011) [LH] Standard unit of count on logistics records Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review App locks unit after first stock record.
ST-02 app metric / app rule Stock on hand (balance) counts only usable stock available for use and excludes expired or damaged items. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] Definition of usable stock on hand Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Expired stock is separated in app & paper cards.
ST-03 app metric / app rule A stockout occurs when usable stock on hand for an item reaches zero. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] Stockout definition and monitoring Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Even if expired batches exist on shelf, 0 usable = stockout.
ST-04 app rule / checklist item Staff perform regular physical counts of visible stock on the shelf and adjust stock records to match actual usable quantities. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] Physical inventory count and record reconciliation Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Supported by Page 2 of stock sheet and app Count entry.
ST-05 app rule All stock losses and adjustments are recorded separately from issues and require an explicit reason code (Expired – removed, Damaged, Lost, Transferred out, Found, Transferred in, Correction). Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] Recording losses and adjustments on stock records Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Distinguishes consumption (issues) from losses.
ST-06 app rule / checklist item Stock cards and app records capture date, receipts, issues, physical counts, losses/adjustments, usable balance, batch number, and expiry date. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] Essential data elements on stockkeeping records / bin cards Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Area code is optional on issues.
ST-07 app rule Supplies that expire are managed by first-to-expire, first-out (FEFO): the earliest-expiring usable batch is displayed first as 'Use first' and automatically deducted first on issue. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] First-to-expire, first-out (FEFO) warehouse/storeroom rule Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Reduces wastage from expiry.
ST-10 app rule / checklist item Expired stock never counts toward usable stock totals and must be removed from usable storage; an expiry date is treated as usable up to and including that calendar date. Published guidance USAID | DELIVER PROJECT, The Logistics Handbook 2011 [LH] Handling expired products and usable stock calculation Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Inclusive expiry date boundary is explicit in app logic.
RC-01 checklist item Safe water is available in patient care areas for handwashing and environmental cleaning, with covered backup water storage where piped supply is intermittent. Published guidance WHO/UNICEF WASH FIT (2nd ed., 2022); WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Water domain indicators; Core Component 8 (Built environment, materials and equipment for IPC) Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Readiness checklist Section 1.
RC-02 checklist item Functional hand hygiene stations with soap and running water or alcohol-based hand rub are available at points of care and within 5 metres of toilets. Published guidance WHO/UNICEF WASH FIT (2nd ed., 2022); WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Hand hygiene domain indicators; Core Component 8 Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Readiness checklist Sections 2 and 3.
RC-03 checklist item Environmental cleaning supplies (detergent, disinfectant, mops/buckets, utility gloves) and a clear cleaning routine are available and followed. Published guidance WHO/UNICEF WASH FIT (2nd ed., 2022); WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Environmental cleanliness domain; Core Component 8 Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Readiness checklist Section 4.
RC-04 checklist item Health care waste is segregated at the point of generation (such as a three-bin system for general, infectious, and sharps waste) and safely stored/treated. Published guidance WHO/UNICEF WASH FIT (2nd ed., 2022); WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Health care waste management domain; Core Component 8 Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Readiness checklist Section 5.
RC-05 checklist item Sharps are placed immediately into puncture-resistant sharps containers located within arm's reach and replaced before passing the three-quarters fill line. Published guidance WHO/UNICEF WASH FIT (2nd ed., 2022) Sharps waste safety indicators Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Readiness checklist item 5.2.
RC-06 checklist item / app rule Improvement action plans assign each corrective action to a responsible role with a due date and review completion at the next review meeting. Published guidance WHO/UNICEF WASH FIT (2nd ed., 2022) Incremental improvement plan & review cycle Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Kit uses roles (never personal names) for accountability.
IPC-01 app rule / checklist item Regular IPC monitoring, audit of practices and supplies, and timely feedback to staff and leadership are core requirements of an IPC programme. Published guidance WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Core Component 6: Monitoring/audit of IPC practices and feedback Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Basis for monthly review routine.
IPC-02 checklist item / training claim Each facility should have a designated, trained IPC focal person (or lead) responsible for coordinating IPC activities and local reviews. Published guidance WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Core Component 1: IPC programmes (primary care & facility level) Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Role responsible for Part A review and monthly routine.
IPC-03 training claim Effective IPC improvement combines multiple elements (supplies at point of care, training, observation and feedback, reminders, and supportive culture). Published guidance WHO Minimum requirements for IPC programmes 2019 Core Component 5: Multimodal strategies for IPC interventions Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Explains why stock, readiness, and hand hygiene are paired.
PR-01 checklist item The Facility Data-Handling Plan organizes local device, backup, access, and retention rules around administrative, physical, and technical safeguard categories as a reference model, not a claim of legal HIPAA compliance. Published guidance US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) – HIPAA Security Rule Administrative, Physical, and Technical Safeguards (45 CFR Part 164 Subpart C) Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Reference model only; kit stores no ePHI.
PR-02 app rule / checklist item Personal identifiers listed in the HIPAA Safe Harbor de-identification standard (names, ID numbers, exact timestamps, photos, clinical details) are excluded from all app fields and paper sheets. Published guidance US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) – HIPAA Privacy Rule Safe Harbor de-identification method (45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)) Not yet verified against source text – check during local IPC lead review Used to verify that no identifying fields exist.
PR-03 app rule The IPC Starter Kit stores no personal health information (PHI) or staff identifiers; any future workflow that collects PHI requires a separate deployment-specific privacy and security review. Kit design choice IPC Starter Kit architecture & scope boundary Data minimization and zero-PHI scope Kit design choice (verified in schema & forms) Enforced across all HTML forms and JSON/CSV exports.
