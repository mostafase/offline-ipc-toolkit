IPC Starter Kit – One-Page Quick Start

What this kit is: A free, fully offline starter kit for infection prevention and control (IPC) in clinics and hospital wards [IPC-01, IPC-03]. It includes a one-file browser app (ipc-kit.html) and printable paper sheets for hand hygiene observation, IPC supply stock tracking, readiness checks, and a 30-minute monthly review.

Privacy Rule: Do not write names, ID numbers or clinical details on this sheet. Never record patient or staff names, ID numbers, clinical details, photos, or free-text observation notes on paper or in the app [HH-10, PR-02, PR-03]. The app saves only grouped counts by MONTH and AREA CODE. Where a responsible person is needed, always write a ROLE (for example, Ward in-charge or IPC focal person), never a person's name [RC-06].

1. How to Open the App

  • Desktop (Windows, macOS, Linux): Double-click ipc-kit.html. No install, no internet, no account, and no admin rights are needed. Keep the folder in one fixed place.
  • Where data lives: Data is saved only in that browser on that device (browser local storage). Clearing browser data, switching browsers, or moving the file shows an empty dataset—use backup files to keep records safe.
  • Top bar checks: Check the mode badge (PRACTICE – fictional data or REAL DATA) and save status (✔ Saved on this device 14:05 or ⚠ Not saved).
  • Android / iPhone / iPad: Unzip first and open ipc-kit.html directly in Chrome/Firefox (avoid Jelly WebView or tapping cross-file links under content://, which cause ERR_FILE_NOT_FOUND). All printable sheets are built inside ipc-kit.html (and field-kit/ALL-PRINTABLE-SHEETS.html). Run Settings > Check this device; if saving fails or on iPhone/iPad Safari, use paper forms.

2. First Launch & 5-Minute Practice Run

On first launch, choose Try a 5-minute practice run (uses fictional data kept separate from real data) or Set up for real use (enter facility-approved area codes first, such as OPD, MAT, WARD-A; capital letters, numbers, or dash, up to 12 characters).

5-minute practice checklist on the Home tab:

  1. Answer the 6 practice questions (~2 min) [HH-09].
  2. Tally 3 opportunities, including one with two moments at the same time (~1 min) [HH-01, HH-02].
  3. Finish the session and read the result (~30 s) [HH-03, HH-06].
  4. Record one stock issue and look at the alerts (~1 min) [ST-06, ST-07].
  5. Make a backup file (~30 s).

3. Everyday Use (App Tabs: Home · Hand hygiene · Stock · Reports · Backup · Settings)

  • Hand hygiene (weekly sessions of ~20 min) [HH-08]: Use Hand hygiene > Tally (pick Month and Area code, tick all moments 1–5 that apply, tap one outcome: Hand rub, Handwash, or Missed; use Undo last to fix a tap, then Finish session and add to totals) OR observe on the paper sheet (hand-hygiene-observation-sheet.html) and enter totals in Hand hygiene > Paper totals [HH-01..HH-06].
  • Stock (as supplies move + monthly count): Check Stock > Status to see usable stock, stockouts, items at/below reorder point, items expiring within the alert window (default 90 days), and expired stock to remove. The earliest-expiring usable batch is marked Use first [ST-02, ST-03, ST-07..ST-10]. Record movements in Stock > Record (Receipt, Issue, Physical count, or Adjustment with reason code) or on stock-card-and-count-sheet.html [ST-01, ST-04..ST-06].
  • Weekly backup: Open Backup and click Download backup file (saves ipc-kit-backup_YYYY-MM-DD_HHMM_v1.json; practice files include PRACTICE). Copy it to the approved backup location in your facility's Data-Handling Plan [PR-01]. If file download fails on your device, use Show backup text and Paste backup text.
  • Monthly review (30 minutes): Open Reports to print or download CSV reports (review any small groups before sharing). Meet using review-routine-and-action-log.html and check area readiness with readiness-checklist.html [HH-12, RC-01..RC-06, IPC-01].

4. Where to Get Help

Ask your facility's IPC focal person or Ward in-charge [IPC-02]. Before starting real data collection, the IPC focal person must complete ipc-lead-review-and-pilot-checklist.html and data-handling-plan.html.

30-Minute Monthly Review Routine (for the IPC Focal Person) [HH-12, RC-06, IPC-01]

  1. Prepare report before the meeting: In Reports, select the month and area codes, print the summary or download the CSV reports, and bring completed readiness-checklist.html sheets and stock alerts [IPC-01].
  2. Check last month's actions (5 min): Fill in Checked on, Done? (Yes/Partly/No), and Result seen next review on last month's log rows [RC-06].
  3. Share results with counts, not just percentages (10 min): Always present both numbers, e.g. 160 of 200 opportunities, 80% [HH-03, HH-06]. Combine sessions by adding counts first, never by averaging percentages [HH-05].
  4. Flag small samples: Any group with fewer than 200 opportunities (or your facility's threshold) is flagged Small sample – interpret with care [HH-07]. Review small groups before sharing reports outside the team.
  5. Discuss without blaming individuals (5 min): Observation is for improving supplies, placement, and workflow—never for naming or punishing staff [HH-10, HH-12, IPC-03].
  6. Pick at most 3 actions & assign to a ROLE with due date (10 min): Choose up to 3 realistic fixes. Assign each to a ROLE (IPC focal person, Ward in-charge, Storekeeper, Facility manager, Maintenance lead, or Cleaning supervisor—never a name) with a clear due date. Hand Hygiene Practice Scenarios – Trainer Answer Key

Keep separate during group quiz · Discuss each rule [HH-01..HH-11]
Do not write names, ID numbers or clinical details on this sheet. [HH-10, PR-02, PR-03]
Scenario 1 Answer: Moments 4 and 1 (4 After touching a patient + 1 Before touching a patient), Outcome: Hand rub, counted as ONE opportunity [HH-01, HH-02, HH-04].
Why: Moving directly from touching one patient to touching the next creates two indications at the same time. Two moments at the same time count once as a single hand hygiene opportunity (one row on the observation sheet).
Scenario 2 Answer: Moment 2 (Before a clean/aseptic procedure), Outcome: Missed [HH-01, HH-04, HH-11].
Why: Dressing a wound is a clean/aseptic procedure. Putting on gloves does not replace hand hygiene; hands must be cleaned before putting on gloves.
Scenario 3 Answer: Moment 3 (After body fluid exposure risk), Outcome: Handwash [HH-01, HH-04, HH-11].
Why: Emptying a urine drainage bag carries a body fluid exposure risk. Hands must be cleaned immediately after removing gloves; washing with soap and water is recorded as Handwash.
Scenario 4 Answer: Moment 5 (After touching patient surroundings), Outcome: Missed [HH-01, HH-04].
Why: Touching the patient's bed sheets without touching the patient is Moment 5. Leaving the patient zone without cleaning hands is a missed opportunity.
Scenario 5 Answer: b) Area A has too few observations to compare; observe more before drawing conclusions. [HH-06, HH-07]
Why: Always read the counts alongside the percentage. Five opportunities is a very small sample (one missed action changes the result by 20 percentage points). Results with fewer than 200 opportunities are flagged Small sample – interpret with care.
Scenario 6 Answer: b) 19 of 40 opportunities, 48% [HH-03, HH-05, HH-06].
Why: Sessions are combined by adding the counts first (9 + 10 = 19 actions; 10 + 30 = 40 opportunities; 100 × 19 / 40 = 47.5%, rounded to 48%), never by averaging the session percentages.
Exact Moment Labels Used in App and Paper Forms [HH-01]:
  1. 1 Before touching a patient
  2. 2 Before a clean/aseptic procedure
  3. 3 After body fluid exposure risk
  4. 4 After touching a patient
  5. 5 After touching patient surroundings
